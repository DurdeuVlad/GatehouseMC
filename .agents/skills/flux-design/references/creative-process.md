# Creative process for originating a design

Creativity here is treated as a repeatable process, not a moment of inspiration. Good design rarely comes from being first with an idea — it comes from recombining existing, proven elements in a way that fits the problem at hand.

1. **Know the basics.** Apply [design-principles.md](design-principles.md) before anything else — the process below assumes those checks are already part of the habit.
2. **Gather concrete inspiration before drawing anything.** Look at existing top-tier sites or apps solving a similar problem, a relevant Figma community file, or a curated pattern library, filtered to the same category as the problem (e.g. "testimonial section" + "finance app"). Collect two or three references, not one — a single reference gets copied, several get synthesized.
3. **Study the references as a user, not a designer.** Note what specifically works (tone, structure, level of detail) and what to avoid (genericness, missing emotion, clutter), before forming an initial direction.
4. **Draft an initial pass**, applying the design-principles checklist as you go.
5. **Step away before finalizing.** Incubation is a real step, not a delay: putting the problem down and returning to it later surfaces new angles that forcing the work does not. If ideas still don't come after stepping away, treat that as a signal to check sleep/stress rather than to keep forcing the same draft.
6. **Don't fall in love with the first draft.** Show it to a colleague or friend before showing it to real users; be ready to discard structural choices, not just polish, based on feedback. A later attempt often only keeps one strong piece (e.g. one good section) from an earlier one — that's expected, not wasted effort.
7. **Ship something rather than iterating in your head indefinitely.** A finished, imperfect draft that can collect real feedback beats an unfinished perfect one. Always label an unreviewed result as a draft (see the parent skill's Output contract) rather than presenting it as done.
